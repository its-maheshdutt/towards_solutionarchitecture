Field Watch v0: your first ship in 7 days

A satellite look at one field near Tirupati, with plain-language notes in Telugu and English. Small enough for a week, and it touches geo, cloud-style data access and AI at once.

  1. Draw a small polygon around one field in QGIS and save it as GeoJSON.
  2. Pull a year of Sentinel-2 images for it from a public STAC API (Earth Search or Microsoft Planetary Computer), cloud cover under 20%.
  3. Compute NDVI for each date and plot the season as a line.
  4. Add daily rainfall and temperature from Open-Meteo or NASA POWER on the same chart.
  5. Send the numbers (not the images) to an LLM and ask for a five-line summary in Telugu and English, with the numbers printed beside it.
  6. Publish the notebook and one screenshot, and note what surprised you.

Public data only. This is a learning demo, not agronomic advice: check anything you would act on with a local agronomist.

Checked, not guessed

What the 2026 market and the research actually say

Frameworks age out in a couple of years. The reasoning underneath them does not. So the plan weights the parts that outlast any one tool (math, data-structure and database theory, distributed-systems trade-offs, geodesy) at least as heavily as the tool of the month. Phase 1 is written to that rule.

Market signals (2026)

Engineering, not analysis, is where the pay is. Across 343 disclosed 2026 postings, geospatial software and data-engineering roles top every other geospatial job family, ahead of GIS analysis, cartography and remote sensing. Python, cloud platforms and backend skills are named as the drivers of the jump to senior pay.

Analysis is moving into the cloud warehouse. Continental-scale spatial analytics increasingly runs inside cloud data platforms instead of being exported to desktop GIS. That is what phases 3 and 4 are for.

Open data is consolidating around AI grounding. Overture Maps' entity IDs (GERS) are being adopted so AI pipelines can point to verified real-world places instead of guessing. Microsoft reports measurable address-accuracy gains from switching.

Digital twins are becoming a compliance requirement for asset-heavy sectors, which includes the utility clients you already serve. Phase 4 is built around one.

Sources: GEO CAREERS, Geospatial Salaries 2026 · BioMedware, 5 Geospatial Trends for 2026 · Overture Maps Foundation, 50-member release

Academic frontier

The field has moved from "build a model" to "adapt one." Remote-sensing foundation models (Clay, Prithvi and others built on vision transformers) are pretrained starting points that get fine-tuned or prompted per task. A 2025 survey of multimodal geospatial foundation models tracks the shift from few-shot to zero-shot transfer.

The same survey is direct about the gaps: weak generalization across sensors and resolutions, poor interpretability for high-stakes use, and real compute and privacy limits. A pretrained model fixes none of these for you, which is an argument for knowing what is under the hood.

Location is becoming a learned feature. Geo-embedding models represent coordinates as context-aware vectors instead of raw lat/long. They drop into ordinary classification and regression, so they are a cheap, durable skill (Phase 6).

Sources: Survey of Multimodal Geospatial Foundation Models (arXiv 2510.22964) · Esri ArcNews, GeoAI in the Age of Foundation Models · Geo-Foundation Models: Reality, Gaps and Opportunities (ACM SIGSPATIAL)

Engineering in the AI age

Routine build work is being handed to agents. One 2026 prediction piece (Datafold, so one informed view and not settled fact) expects agents to take over most routine code writing, debugging and migration work. The skills it expects to grow: context and metadata that agents can use, safe and governed agent access to data, complex SQL written correctly, and working with stakeholders.

Agent-facing interfaces are already shipping in geospatial. A FOSS4G:UK 2026 talk describes a team that moved from a custom chatbot to an MCP server over PostGIS, deployed on Azure Functions, so any AI agent can query their geospatial data safely. That is close to the stack in this roadmap, and Phase 4 has you build the same pattern.

The database vendor is formalising it. Microsoft's DP-800 (SQL AI Developer Associate) covers embeddings, vector and hybrid search and RAG next to T-SQL tuning, security and CI/CD. A training provider's summary puts roughly a quarter to a third of the exam on the AI part. Confirm the weights in Microsoft's own study guide.

Measure, do not trust the feeling. A 2025 randomized trial by METR (16 experienced open-source developers, 246 issues) found tasks took 19% longer with AI allowed, while the developers believed it had made them 20% faster. The tools have improved since and the sample is small, so read it as a reason to keep your own timing log. The figures come from a secondary write-up, so check the original paper before quoting them.

Working with AI

Four rules for the AI age

AI is a thread through all six phases, not a topic saved for the end. Each phase has a teal AI block with its own checklist. These four rules govern all of them.

Rule 1

You own the design and the tests

Write the spec, the data contract and the failing test yourself, then let an agent draft the code. If you cannot explain a diff, do not merge it.

Rule 2

Measure, do not feel

Time a task with and without AI and count the defects. Keep the log. Your own numbers beat any headline, including the ones above.

Rule 3

Learn it first, then speed it up

Derive the CRS transformation by hand before asking a tool. Use AI as a tutor that quizzes you, not as a way to skip the lesson. The fundamentals are what let you judge its output.

Rule 4

Design for swapping models

Put every model call behind a small interface so you can change vendor or size, or run an open-weight model locally for sensitive data. Models will change several times during this roadmap.

AI hygiene at Spatial Eye

A working summary of your employer's AI policy (v2.2). The full text on the IT/Asset Management SharePoint site is the authority, so re-read it before you start building.

  • Prototype with public or synthetic data only. Customer network data, exports and databases stay out of AI tools unless a written customer agreement exists.
  • Clean logs, stack traces and configs first. Replace host names, IP addresses, user names, customer names and credentials with placeholders.
  • Never paste secrets. No passwords, API keys, tokens or connection strings with credentials, in any tool.
  • You verify the output. Code, figures and contract text are yours to check before anyone relies on them.
  • Pick the right tool. Claude is for software development and technical problem-solving. Mail, documents, meetings and Teams content go to Microsoft 365 Copilot.

Cashing it in

Turning the build log into a paycheck

Skills only pay if the market can see them. A short, plain note on where this combination sits and how to make it visible.

Why this combination is scarce

Most GIS specialists stop at desktop tools and never touch orchestration, cloud infrastructure as code or AI engineering. Most data engineers have no spatial reasoning and would not spot a projection error that corrupts a dashboard. You are building the thin overlap: domain depth (utility networks, Dutch spatial standards, urban planning), a modern data platform, and the ability to put AI into products and into your own workflow safely. The overlap, not any single skill, is what commands a premium. As routine coding gets cheaper, judgment, domain knowledge and trustworthy systems matter more.

Titles worth targeting as the roadmap progresses:

  • Geospatial Data Engineer: pipelines and cloud, spatial-aware
  • Spatial Data Platform / Architecture Engineer: after phases 3 and 4
  • Geo-AI Engineer: after Phase 6, still a rare title
  • Geospatial Solutions / DaaS Product Engineer: matches your Spatial Eye trajectory directly

The clearest hard number comes from North America, where postings disclose pay more consistently. A 2026 analysis of 343 salary-disclosing geospatial postings puts Geospatial Software & Data Engineering at a $167,250 median, the highest of eight categories, with Cloud/DevSecOps second at $150,000. Dutch and EU figures run lower in nominal terms, but there is no reason to expect the pattern (engineering and cloud skills outearning GIS-analyst skills in the same field) to stop at the Atlantic.

Check current EU ranges yourself rather than trusting a fixed number here: GEO CAREERS, Geospatial Salaries 2026, Glassdoor Amsterdam data engineer, PayScale Netherlands, SalaryExpert Netherlands.

Make it visible

Record a 60-second demo for every build. A short screen capture, a repo with an architecture diagram, and a screenshot of the map beat a list of tool names. Pin the builds from phases 2, 5 and 6 on GitHub.

Publish your evals, failures included. A page showing where your agent gets things wrong reads as seniority, because most AI demos hide it.

Write the build, not only the code. A short case study per build, covering the problem, the design choices and what broke, shows how you think.

Use your real story. "I learned by building things from my own life, and I measured where AI helped and where it did not" is specific and believable. Lean on it in interviews.

Certify selectively. DP-203 and DP-800 are worth the exam fees because recruiters filter on them. Everything else is better proven by a repo than a badge.
